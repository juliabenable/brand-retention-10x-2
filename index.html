Brand retention · KPIs, competition, segments, and the 10x

What they'd fight for

Tony's test is the right one: a brand should fight someone who tells them they can't keep subscribing. Today five of fourteen chose to leave instead, all inside 95 days. This write-up says why, what the field does about it, which numbers to run the company on, and where the 10x is. The 10x is not a longer feature list. It is a guarantee we can keep, sold to the segment that measures us on it.

The short version

Six things to know before Friday

1 · Where we are

The leak is now the constraint on the $1M, not sales.

5 of 14 paying brands have cancelled or paused, all between day 47 and day 93. That is 13.8% monthly logo churn against a 3% median for SaaS at our price. At that rate, 8 new brands a month tops out at 58 brands. The $1M needs 119 at $700, or 42 at $2,000.

2 · Why they leave

They leave for one of two reasons, and never for price.

Three churned because the contracted creators did not show up (offer too thin, creators declined, creators ghosted after receiving product). Two churned because they were sold sales and we delivered content. Nobody left over price, bugs, or Katie.

3 · The KPI

One number at the top: creators delivered against creators promised, per brand, per month.

It is the literal contract, it is visible before the brand decides, and it is upstream of four of the five churns. Under it sits a short stack that differs by segment. Sales is a KPI for one segment only.

4 · The field

Competitors retain with four mechanics we have none of.

Guaranteed output (Statusphere, Stack Influence), a rights-included content library, ad-activation plumbing, and attribution. The universal churn reason in their reviews is ours too: creators who take the product and vanish, with no recourse.

5 · Segments

Four jobs, three buyer sizes, different physics.

Ops-relief DTC brands retain on delivery and proof. Affiliate-origin brands retain on attributable sales and we are 0 for 2. Local businesses retain on footfall and are cheap to serve but priced at the floor. Mid-market programs (PCA, Pair, Abercrombie) retain on reporting and procurement fit.

6 · The 10x

Sell a guarantee, then a ladder, in that order.

Guarantee delivered posts with a remedy, price it into Sep 15. Make the roster and rights library the thing that accumulates. Put a tracked link on every gifted creator. Run local as its own free-first motion. Do the founder calls now, in a switch-interview shape.

Why five left, why nine stay

Two failure modes, one of them ours to fix this month

Every churn is an autopsy of a gate in the Aug 29 model. Three died at delivery, two at fit. What matters now is that the delivery deaths include a segment we thought was safe.

Dips Kitchen · $150 · day 67Gate 0 and 3

Offer too thin to fill the contract

A comped lunch does not move creators. Acceptance collapsed, one campaign ran in three paid months, and the founder read the thin pool as our failure to source bigger names. Nothing in the product ever told him the constraint was his offer.

Killed by: an offer we accepted at sale and never graded.

Pecan Moon · $399 · day 47Gate 1 and 2

Desirability we could not predict

High product value, creators still declined. No seeded sales either, so the portal showed nothing. Katie: "we did the same thing there, and yet we're having an issue with creators didn't want to accept even though it was a much higher value product." [Q]

Killed by: low acceptance plus an empty proof surface.

Celeste Naturals · $499 · day 78Gate 1

Creators took the product and vanished

Contracted at 20 creators a month. Karen Franco: 37 reminders, zero replies, product received Jul 30 and Aug 9. Gabriella: 36 reminders, one reply, nothing since. Hikatbeauty 38 reminders. Tessy 11. Camila 12. The campaign was archived with those rows still open. [L]

Killed by: ghosting after receipt, with no replacement and no remedy. This is a segment A brand.

Urthy Scents · $399 · day 81Gate 0 and 2

Sold an outcome the model does not produce, then went quiet

"Where are my sales?" on Jul 17, in Katie's account. Affiliate-origin buyer measuring us against a cost per sale. Content delivered, promise not. Then a month of silence on both sides, until Tony asked on Aug 17 whether to stop billing and had Brian cancel it the same day. [Q]

Killed by: the sales promise, and nobody calling when the brand went dark.

Mojo Fun · $399 · day 93Gate 0 and 2

The agency's client wanted revenue

Chris at Clockwork Affiliates signed on the brand's behalf and is paid on performance. Our smoothest campaign, one wrap report by Aug 27, one seeded sale. He asked about end-of-three-month timing on Jul 27 and emailed to cancel the week of Aug 25. Tony refunded month four and paused. [Q][L]

Killed by: an intermediary buyer with a CPA scorecard, plus proof arriving once per campaign.

The pattern5 of 5

Delivery or fit. Never price, never bugs, never Katie.

Three churns are the creators-did-not-show family (Dips, Pecan, Celeste). Two are the sold-on-sales family (Urthy, Mojo). All five happened inside the window the field calls the activation window. The Moxo 2026 B2B report puts 43% of client churn inside the first 90 days; ours is 100%. [W]

Fix delivery for everyone. Decide whether to sell to CPA buyers at all.

Three structural facts the ledger could not show

Billing starts before value does

Katie: "their billing has already started. So by the time they are launching that first campaign they might already be a week in." Julia, on the rule itself: "deciding to do it from first day of billing versus first day of launch campaign was an arbitrary decision that I took. I am also happy to move that. That was not based on industry standards." A brand can be two weeks into paying before it sees anything. [Q]

Nobody measures days paid before first post. It should be on the dashboard by Friday.

A monthly campaign does not finish in a month

Katie: "The design is, you get a campaign a month. That's what you're paying for. The problem is that campaign's not finished in a month." Julia's own count of the elapsed time from invite to post lands at day 28 to 31 in a good case. So the brand is billed for month two before month one has delivered. [Q]

Campaign length is about eight weeks today. Four is the target.

The dead zone after shipping

Katie: "I invited creators, they accepted, I shipped packages, to then nothing really until your first piece of content is here. How do we keep them warm, engaged and excited about what's coming?" Gate 2 in this study said proof arrives per campaign while billing is monthly. Katie's version is sharper: there is a specific silent stretch, right after the brand has done all its work. [Q]

No proof event fires between shipping and the first post.

The clock they built on the call

Julia: "we need a view with the clock for every single brand, day 0, day 4, day 14, and having us track what we have achieved by then." Katie: "By day 7 this should be where we are at. If we are not, then what do we need to do to get back to that magical experience?"

Day 4

And either the campaign launched or the onboarding call held. Katie: "on day 4 is either launch campaign or onboarding call."

Day 7

Julia: "if we only care about the first one, we can cheat our way to the first one, which is fine, because we're trying to show them value earlier on." That is not gaming the metric. It is recognising that the first post is the product demo.

Where this changes what this study recommended

Sequencing: front end before back end

This study put the Creator Guarantee and ghost-proofing first. Katie's view is that by the time ghosting bites, the brand has already decided: "the credit card thing is useful, but by then I don't even think they care. They're so over it and annoyed with the first 75% that it's just, let's wrap it up. So I think we're solving for the wrong part of the process."

Revision: the guarantee still ships with the Sep 15 pricing, because it is a sales asset and a management tool. But the first engineering hours go to the first fourteen days, not to the card hold.

The wrap is not the proof that matters

Gate 2 in this study leaned on the wrap and the monthly statement. Julia on the call: "I don't think there's an amount of wrap that we can send that will change their mind." Katie: "Completely agree." Katie also noted the brands often see the content on social before we tell them: "as soon as they get that tag on social, they're often already seeing that piece of content before we're even telling them. So we're behind the ball there."

Revision: proof means a per-event feed inside the first two weeks, not a document at the end. Both committed to sending one manually every other day next week.

Sales is confirmed as the wrong headline

Section 7 of this study argued that the payout and sales work retains segment B only. Katie put it harder: "we're never going to get 30 sales. That's not even worth solving for."

Unchanged, and reinforced. Sales stays a segment B metric.

Design for the picky brand, not the easy one

Katie: "I would almost rather plan as if the brands are going to be extremely picky. I would much rather blow up the system for all of that, because that's going to be 99% of our brands." Julia agreed. This study's segment model still holds, but the build target moves to the harder end of segment A.

Revision: the roster and matching flow are designed for the Roguery, not for MD Solar.

Open applications alongside matching, limited to creators who have already completed a campaign. What competitors do to keep a brand

Nobody sells managed service at our price. Everybody who retains sells one of four things we don't.

Two research passes across 22 platforms, public sources only: pricing pages, help centers, Trustpilot, Capterra, Shopify App Store, press. No platform publishes churn or NPS, so the evidence is what brands say when they leave and what the winners choose to guarantee. Full source list in the method section.

Where we sit. Social Cat sells self-serve gifting at $99 to $299 a month, cancel anytime, no delivery guarantee, and states in writing that it "does not offer refunds if creators fail to publish content." Statusphere sells guaranteed posts per month, hands-off, at roughly $3,500 a month and up. Stack Influence charges $30 to $39 only when a post goes live and the creator buys the product from the brand's own store. Benable sells the Statusphere promise at the Social Cat price, with Katie in the middle absorbing the risk both of them price for. That is the wedge, and it is also why every no-show lands on our P&L and our retention. [W]

The retention mechanics that recur across the platforms brands stay on

1 · Risk moved off the brand: pay only for output

Stack Influence charges only on a completed post; a creator who orders and does not post is not reimbursed and the brand is not charged. Statusphere sells "guaranteed creator posts, not platform seats." Insense refunds the subscription if fewer than five matching creators apply. Trend's old page: "If they don't you will be reimbursed."

Statusphere · Stack Influence · Insense · Trend/soona · Bazaarvoice ("85% content completion within eight weeks")

Benable: none. Contract promises N creators, remedy undefined.

2 · Rights included, content library by default

Perpetual or unlimited usage is table stakes everywhere in the managed tier. inBeat lists "licensing windows and renewal costs" as the levers agencies use at renewal. Kynship left seeding-only because gifted content is worth most as paid-ads creative.

Insense · Stack · minisocial · soona · Statusphere · Cohley · JoinBrands · Social Cat

Benable: 30-day rights per post, extension paid per creator, no library surface. Chiara kept Social Cat for exactly this.

3 · Ad-activation plumbing

One-click Spark Ads, Meta Partnership Ads and whitelisting codes at Insense, JoinBrands, Statusphere, Cohley. The Shelf's client: "Whitelisting ads having 7x engagement rate of ads from our brand pages." This turns a collab into paid-media inventory, which is what makes the spend defensible at month 3.

Insense · JoinBrands · Statusphere · Cohley · #paid (whitelisting licenses sold per creator)

Benable: none.

4 · Attribution that makes results visible

ShopMy: "brands can see exactly who made a conversion," cited as the core reason budgets move there. Cohley reports revenue per asset. Shopify Collabs shows sales per creator. Platforms without attribution get "zero results" churn even when content was delivered.

ShopMy · LTK · Levanta · Shopify Collabs · Cohley · Superfiliate

Benable: tracked links exist for creators, brand-facing attribution is per campaign and thin; seeded sales unlabelled.

5 · A named human on the account

Reviews that stay name a person: "Lana" at Upfluence, "Tihi" at JoinBrands, "Brett" at Afluencer, "feels like an extension of our team" at Statusphere. Reviews that leave say "reached out three times, no response." Every platform sells the human as the top tier: Social Snowball $899, #paid $999, Insense $500, Impact $2,500.

Aspire · Upfluence · Later · Statusphere · Ubiquitous (daily Slack)

Benable: Katie. This is the asset. It is sold as the base, not the tier.

6 · Two-sided accountability and replacement

Social Cat rates brands and creators both ways and sells "influencer replacement" and "Replace Matches." Trend and Shopify Collabs reviewers cite the absence of a rating or flag system as the reason creators ghost: "No repercussions for creators not fulfilling their end."

Social Cat · Collabstr (escrow) · Stack (no reimbursement if no post)

Benable: card-hold shipping this month; no replacement promise to the brand yet.

7 · Always-on cadence, not 30-day resets

Cohley users churn because they "could not continually run campaigns rather than sporadic campaigns." HireInfluence sells credits across 6 to 12 months to cut lead time "from 5 or 6 weeks down to 8 or 10 days." Statusphere sells a monthly guaranteed count.

HireInfluence · Statusphere · Cohley (3-month pilot to annual)

Benable: billed monthly, campaigns run 6 to 9 weeks, proof arrives per campaign.

8 · Creator relationships living inside the platform

ShopMy: "70% of brands onboard their own creators and 20% of brand leads come from creator referrals." LTK's asset is 44M shoppers in its app. Once roster, links and payouts live inside, leaving means re-onboarding creators.

ShopMy · LTK · Aspire (CRM) · Levanta

Benable: creator pool and rehire exist in code, invisible to the brand.

9 · Money-denominated reporting and time saved

Cohley: revenue per asset, AOV. Bazaarvoice: EMV and "34 hours saved." Statusphere: "save 98% of the time." Reach-only dashboards correlate with "hit or miss" reviews and "could not afford enough credits to make a difference."

Cohley · Bazaarvoice · Shopify Collabs · Statusphere

Benable: wrap report per campaign, released manually, no monthly statement.

On the Aug 27 term change. Annual billed monthly with 90-day notice puts us in the $2,000-plus bracket's contract with the $400 bracket's price. SaaS Capital's data says month-to-month "is more compelling as a potential driver of revenue growth than any downside it might have on customer churn," and ChartMogul's 2024 report shows annual plans carrying about 10 points higher retention at our ARPA, so both readings have evidence. The term is defensible only if the guarantee arrives with it. A brand that gives 90 days notice at day 60 because five creators ghosted will pay three more months and tell every founder they know. Where the field is going in 2025 and 2026

  • AI for ops, not AI for content: Statusphere's StevieOps and SmartMatch, Cohley's AI asset analysis, Bazaarvoice Vibe. The target is the brand's hidden hours in sourcing, vetting and QA. This is what Katie's SMS and matching machine already is. [W]
  • Gifting plus affiliate on one subscription: Insense and Statusphere are TikTok Shop partners; JoinBrands added GMV-tiered bonuses in July; Aspire reports $52M in attributed affiliate sales; Social Cat's own report has affiliate at 11% of posts. [W]
  • Local productized: JoinBrands launched Local Jobs in July 2026, in-person creator visits with scheduling. First marketplace move into our local lane. [W]
  • Consolidation and ladders: Trend folded into soona, Afluencer bought by Influencer Hero as its top of funnel, Levanta bought Perch+, Statusphere raised $18M in January. Entry tools become on-ramps to a managed or attribution tier. [W]
  • Subsidised lock-in: Cohley's $10M creator fund tied to annual partnerships; JoinBrands monthly credit-back. [W]

How they keep score themselves

The most common self-reported metric, by a distance, is a follow-through ratio quoted like a batting average: "11 influencers over 2 months and 4 of them received the product but never did a video." "100 creators invited, only two responded positively." "Less than 50% conversion between sending the products and the influencer making the agreed upon content." That number is Delivered vs Promised. Brands already compute it by hand, in anger, at cancellation. We should compute it first, calmly, every month, and send it to them. Second is sales attributed by hand; third is usable content pieces; fourth is engagement with a fake-check; fifth is hours saved. What differs by type and size

Local food and service

Posture is defensive: inundated with inbound asks, doubtful of the mechanism, and burned where the unit cost is real. "We will never give you a free ice cream in exchange for a post." "Better avoid this if you have a local business that offers something that really costs." What they value is a human who screens and local relevance. This matches Tony's Aug 26 doubt and argues for local as its own free-first motion, not the core subscription. [W][Q]

DTC product, high cost of goods

The most burned segment. "My products are costly and handcrafted with gold-filled materials, I cannot simply give more items to another influencer." Roguery's $200 jewelry and Pecan Moon's resortwear are this brand. The guarantee and replacement matter most here. [W]

Beauty and skincare

Most crowded creator supply, which cuts both ways. Beauty brands with teams are the happiest done-for-you customers in the corpus; beauty solo founders report the nastiest disputes. Our best references (MD Solar, 28 Litsea, Pholk) and our biggest program (PCA) are all here. [W]

Solo founder vs team vs marketing department

Solo founders feel every unit and hour personally; the register is betrayal, and they churn after one bad month. Two-to-ten person teams buy hours back and tolerate variance if volume is there. Marketing teams want control: hand-picking, raw files without overlays, trackers, PDF briefs for legal. [W]

What keeps them paying for years

  • A small roster of repeat creators they trust ("the same 10 people, over and over"). Agency framing: "find those five people before you build a program around fifty strangers."
  • A content library that keeps filling, downloadable in bulk, with rights.
  • A cadence tied to launches and seasons, month over month, not a one-off.
  • A named human who takes ownership when something breaks. Where a person intervened, the reviewer stayed or softened; where support was a template, they left and warned others.
  • Fair economics with no traps: flexible plans, credits that do not expire, nothing billed before the first result.

The brands who stay are not the ones who got the most reach. They are the ones who never felt alone with the risk. That sentence is the product strategy.

The 10x

A guarantee, then a ladder, then a second motion. In that order.

Tony's frame: "what we need to improve to better retain people and what we should try and 10x, what we need to build." Here is the answer as a sequence, because a 10x offering sold on top of 13.8% churn is a faster leak.

The offer: what to eliminate, reduce, raise and create

Eliminate

  • Unlabelled seeded sales on brand-facing surfaces
  • Campaign as the unit for local businesses
  • Proof that only arrives at wrap
  • Selling gifting on a sales promise

Reduce

  • Brand effort to zero: draft orders, auto fulfilment, no CSV
  • Time to first live post: under 21 days
  • Campaign length: 9 weeks to 4
  • Reminders per creator: card-hold and replacement instead of 38 texts

Raise

  • Delivery certainty: guaranteed posts with a remedy
  • Rights: 30 days included becomes a library with a paid 90-day and perpetual tier
  • Repeat creators: the roster as the spine of the portal
  • Katie as a named account lead, sold as such

Create

  • The Creator Guarantee
  • A monthly statement on the billing date, whether or not a campaign wrapped
  • A tracked link and commission on every gifted creator, labelled real
  • The free creator-assessment link for local (Tony's idea)
  • An invoice-first lane and a monthly business review for programs

The one thing to 10x first

The Creator Guarantee

Promise: "Your plan includes N creator posts live every month. If a creator takes your product and does not post, we replace them at our cost. If fewer than N are live by month end, the shortfall rolls into next month and you do not pay for it." Wording for legal to tighten; the shape is the point.

Why it is the 10x: it is the single line no competitor at our price can say (Social Cat states the opposite in its help center), it is what Statusphere charges $3,500 a month for, it addresses the number one churn reason in the whole category and in our own ledger, and it forces us to run Delivered vs Promised because we are now paying for it. Hart's rule: the guarantee fixes the operation before it sells the product.

What it costs us: the replacement creator and the occasional credit. At Celeste's 5 ghosts on 20 that would have been a quarter of a month rolled forward, not a $499 cancellation and a reference lost.

Where it goes: into the Sep 15 pricing. "More expensive and guaranteed" sells. "More expensive" does not. It is also the only thing that makes annual-with-90-day-notice fair to the brand.

Resequenced after the Sep 2 Katie call. The order below was written to close the delivery gates for brands we already have. Katie's argument, which I accept, is that the brands we are about to lose are lost in their first two weeks, and every brand we sign from here runs that gauntlet. So the first two weeks now lead the month, and the card hold moves behind them. The guarantee still ships on Sep 15 because it is a sales asset and it forces the operation to fix delivery, but it is not what a new brand feels first.

Who

The timeline, not the wishlist

First thought: what was happening the week you first looked for help with creators? Passive looking: what else did you try or consider? Active looking: what made you pick us? The decision: what did you expect in month one? Consuming: what actually happened, week by week? Satisfaction: what would you fight to keep, and what would you not miss?

The four questions that map to the model

1. "If you could no longer use Benable, how would you feel?" (very / somewhat / not disappointed). 2. "What is the main benefit you get?" 3. "What kind of person do you think benefits most?" 4. "What is the one thing that would make it a no-brainer to keep?" Vohra's four, verbatim, so the answers are comparable across brands.

What we listen for

The job (time, sales, footfall, program). The number they screenshot for their boss. The moment they doubted us (a ghosted creator, a silent month, a wrong product shipped). The competitor they still pay for and why (Chiara and Social Cat). The rights, ads, or reporting they would pay more for.

What we bring

Their own Delivered vs Promised number, their content, and one proposal specific to their segment. For the churned five: no pitch, an apology where one is owed, and the guarantee as a reason to come back in November.

One page per brand in the account record: job, success definition, fight-for-it answer, main benefit, one thing. Rolled up for the Oct 1 standup as the segment evidence Tony asked to show the team.
